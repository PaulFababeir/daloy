How many cases will this month's rain bring?

Rainfall is known within days. Case reports arrive 2–3 weeks late. DALOY uses the rain to estimate this month's cases now, so health offices can prepare before the reports arrive.

          How DALOY works
          
          
          Two simple, checkable models. Each one turns a month's rainfall into an expected case count, and each was tested on years it never saw.
          
        
          
      
          

      
          
        
          1
          Collect
          Weekly case counts and rainfall for one city.
          
        
          
        
          2
          Aggregate
          Sum to monthly totals. Weekly data was dominated by a few outbreak weeks.
          
        
          
        
          3
          Model
          Rainfall (and season for leptospirosis) in, expected cases out.
          
        
          
        
          4
          Validate
          Predict each year from all other years. Compare with a baseline.
          
      
          

      
          
        
          
          Dengue · Quezon City
          
          Poisson regression
          
          
          log E[cases] = β₀ + β₁ · z(log(1 + rain))
          
          
          
            
          Input
          Same-month rainfall (RF_NASA, NASA GPM IMERG)
          
            
          Target
          Monthly cases (DC_QC)
          
            
          Fit
          PoissonRegressor(alpha=0.1)
          
            
          Data
          UPRI-NOAH / Pelitro et al. (2026), 2010–2025
          
            
          Effect size
          Unpenalized model, rain in 100 mm units → change in cases per +100 mm
          
          
          
        
          
        
          
          Leptospirosis · Manila
          
          Gradient boosting, Poisson loss
          
          
          rate = f(log(1 + rain), log(1 + rain last month), sin, cos of month)
          
          
          
            
          Inputs
          This month's and last month's rainfall (NASA POWER), season
          
            
          Target
          Weekly case rate × n_weeks
          
            
          Fit
          HistGradientBoostingRegressor(loss="poisson", max_depth=2)
          
            
          Data
          EIDC / De Stercke et al. (2023), 2001–2012
          
            
          Evidence
          Risk peaks ~2 weeks after heavy rain in Manila (Matsushita et al., 2018)

          Limitations
          
          
            
            
          • Nowcast, not forecast. Estimates use this month's rain, so the lead comes from beating the 2–3 week reporting delay.
            • 
            
          • Two cities only. Other cities need retraining.
            • 
            
          • Monthly resolution; rainfall is the only weather input.
            • 
            
          • Leptospirosis data ends in 2012.
            • 
            
          • 2020–2021 reporting was distorted by COVID-19.
            • 
            
          • Dengue week numbering in the source data is not fully reconciled.
            • 
            
          • Poisson ranges understate uncertainty if counts are overdispersed.
            •
